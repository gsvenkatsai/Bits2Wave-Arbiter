# Conformal Safety Intervals on the Digital Twin (ARBITER v2)

## 1. Overview & Theoretical Guarantee

The Project ARBITER v2 Digital Twin implements **split conformal prediction** to construct non-parametric, distribution-free uncertainty intervals for cellular radio Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):
- `dropped_users`
- `mean_sinr_db`
- `energy_w`
- `interference_db`
- `coverage_ratio`
- `drop_rate_ratio`
- `normalized_energy`
- `normalized_interference`

For any user-specified miscoverage tolerance $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ (default $\alpha = 0.10$ for 90% nominal coverage), the interval $\hat{C}_{1-\alpha}(X)$ satisfies the finite-sample marginal coverage property:

$$\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{n+1} \in \hat{C}_{1-\alpha}(X_{n+1})\right) \ge 1 - \alpha$$

where the quantile is computed over $n$ rolling calibration residuals $R_i = |\hat{Y}_i - Y_i|$ as:

$$q = R_{\left(\lceil (n + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil\right)}$$

and the interval is bounded symmetrically:

$$\hat{C}_{1-\alpha}(X) = [\hat{Y} - q, \; \hat{Y} + q]$$

## 2. Rigorous Scientific Boundaries: Simulator Data vs Real Networks

> [!CAUTION]
> **NO CLAIM ABOUT REAL CELLULAR NETWORKS:**  
> The empirical coverage guarantees documented here hold strictly on data generated by the **discrete-time Cost-231 Hata radio network simulation engine** (`SimulationEngine`).  
> **NO CLAIM IS MADE FOR LIVE PHYSICAL LTE/5G PRODUCTION NETWORKS.** Real macro networks exhibit complex multipath scattering, correlated fast/shadow fading, RF clutter, hardware temperature drifts, and non-Poisson spatial user clustering that depart from simplified analytical models. Deployment onto real base stations requires streaming real-time telemetry calibration over live interfaces.

### Approximate Exchangeability
The mathematical coverage guarantee of conformal prediction fundamentally requires **exchangeability** of data points (e.g. i.i.d. draws). In cellular networks, exchangeability is only **approximately true** because of:
1. **Diurnal Non-Stationarity:** Traffic demand changes continuously between nocturnal quiet periods (e.g., 2 AM) and afternoon busy hours.
2. **Controller Actuation Feedback:** Closed-loop actuations (cell sleeping, load shifting, power boosts) alter the radio environment, dynamically shifting $P(Y \mid X)$.
3. **Temporal Autocorrelation:** Fast/slow fading and mobility induce non-zero autocorrelation across adjacent 100 ms simulation frames.

To mitigate non-stationarity, ARBITER maintains a **rolling calibration buffer** ($N = 200$ residuals) that automatically prunes historical observations and adapts to operational distribution shifts.

## 3. Warm-Up Procedure (`ensure_global_evaluator`)

At startup, the global digital twin evaluator may be uninitialized or uncalibrated. Rather than falling back to static fixtures or mock dictionaries, `ensure_global_evaluator(topology="5_CELL_CORE", warm_up=True)` establishes an honest calibration baseline from real simulator ground truth:

1. **Simulator Spin-up:** Instantiates `SimulationEngine(topology, seed)` and advances 1 tick to reach dynamic steady-state user distribution.
2. **Seeded Scenario Execution:** Samples $N$ (default 60) candidate deltas across the valid actuation space:
   - `TX_POWER_DBM`: $\mathcal{U}[33.0, 46.0]\text{ dBm}$
   - `TILT_DEG`: $\mathcal{U}[2.0, 8.0]^\circ$
   - `SLEEP_STATE`: $\{0.0, 1.0\}$ on non-anchor cells
   - `OFFLOAD_RATIO`: $\mathcal{U}[0.0, 0.4]$
3. **Forked Ground-Truth Comparison:** For each sample, the twin predicts KPIs via `predict_v2(apply_drift=False)`, while a forked independent simulator (`engine.fork()`) applies the deltas and steps the radio environment.
4. **Residual Ingestion:** Residuals $|\hat{Y}_{\text{twin}} - Y_{\text{sim}}|$ for all 8 KPIs are ingested into `ConformalPredictor`.
5. **Budget Compliance:** The entire 60-sample warm-up executes in $\sim 100\text{ ms}$, comfortably within the 3.0-second startup budget.

## 4. What "Uncalibrated" Means (Cold-Start & Conservative Intervals)

The twin is considered **uncalibrated** (`calibrated=False`) whenever:
- Fewer than 30 calibration points exist ($n < 30$, such as before warm-up or during cold start).
- Environmental drift or model divergence triggers an explicit recalibration reset.

### Fail-Safe Behavior When Uncalibrated:
- The system **never silently outputs tight or fictitious intervals**.
- `predict_v2()` returns `TwinPredictionV2` with `calibrated=False` and **wide conservative fallback intervals**:
  - `mean_sinr_db`: $\pm 6.0\text{ dB}$ (total width $12.0\text{ dB}$)
  - `energy_w`: $\pm 15.0\text{ W}$
  - `dropped_users`: $\pm 10.0\text{ users}$
  - `coverage_ratio`: $\pm 0.15$
- Contract C3 `calibration_status()` reports:
  ```json
  {
    "calibrated": false,
    "n_calibration": 0,
    "interval_halfwidth_db": null
  }
  ```
- Decision ladder guards detect `calibrated=False` and invoke conservative arbitration (holding or rejecting borderline actions).
- 3D monitor visualizes uncalibrated uncertainty shells by swelling their radius by +25% and displaying `UNCALIBRATED (GUARD ACTIVE)`.

## 5. Contract C3 Interface (`calibration_status`)

`DigitalTwinEvaluator` and `ConformalPredictor` expose the four-developer Contract C3:

```python
evaluator.calibration_status(kpi="mean_sinr_db", alpha=0.10) -> Dict[str, Any]
```

### Response Schema:
- `calibrated` (`bool`): `True` only when $n_{\text{calibration}} \ge 30$; otherwise `False`.
- `n_calibration` (`int`): Count of retained calibration observations for the specified KPI.
- `interval_halfwidth_db` (`float | None`): The finite-sample conformal quantile $q$ for `mean_sinr_db` when calibrated; strictly `None` when uncalibrated.

## 6. Drift Injection Hook & Verification

For interactive demos and fault-injection testing:
```python
# Inject +10.0 dB bias and 2.0 dB noise onto SINR predictions
evaluator.conformal_predictor.set_drift("mean_sinr_db", bias=10.0, noise_std=2.0)
```
As subsequent telemetry observations are ingested under active drift:
1. Residuals $|\hat{Y}_{\text{drifted}} - Y_{\text{sim}}|$ expand.
2. The finite-sample quantile $q$ automatically increases.
3. `status["interval_halfwidth_db"]` widens measurably (e.g., from $5.4\text{ dB}$ to $> 14\text{ dB}$).
4. The 3D uncertainty shells visibly puff out, signaling elevated operational risk.
