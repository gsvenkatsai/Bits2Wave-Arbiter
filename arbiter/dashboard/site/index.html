// THE NOCTURNAL ANOMALY

The 2 AM Collision Challenge

When autonomous cellular controllers act independently during nocturnal traffic lulls, conflicting objectives trigger catastrophic network blackouts.

Uncoordinated Baseline

Controller Interference & Blackout

Three independent rApps/xApps issue simultaneous commands without shared situational awareness:

  • Energy Saving (ES): Switches Cell B into deep sleep (0 W) to eliminate standby power.
  • Load Balancing (LB): Offloads 40% of users from congested Cell A onto neighboring Cell B.
  • Coverage & Capacity (CC): Attempts to boost transmit power to maintain fringe coverage.
Result: 14 Dropped Calls (100% of offloaded users dropped onto sleeping cell)
ARBITER Coordinated

Delta-Utility Referee Optimization

ARBITER captures concurrent proposals, constructs the conflict graph, and synthesizes a Pareto-optimal joint plan:

  • ES Delayed: Cell B sleep is placed on a timed hold until handover queues fully clear.
  • LB Morphed: User offload ratio is throttled from 40% down to 15% within safe headroom.
  • CC Modulated: Transmit power on Cell A is boosted by +1.1 dB to guarantee SINR ≥ 0 dB.
Result: 0 Dropped Calls • 131.5% Utility Improvement • Full Energy Savings Intact

Conformal Digital Twin Safety Bounds

The Project ARBITER v2 Digital Twin implements split conformal prediction to construct distribution-free uncertainty intervals for cellular radio Key Performance Indicators:

P(Y_{n+1} ∈ C_{1-α}(X_{n+1})) ≥ 1 - α

Exchangeability & Cellular Dynamics: The mathematical coverage guarantee holds under exchangeability between calibration and test distributions. In cellular networks with diurnal non-stationarity and closed-loop controller actuation feedback, ARBITER maintains a rolling calibration window (N = 200 ticks) and enforces cold-start guards (n < 30) to provide honest marginal guarantees without assuming uncalibrated stationarity.
