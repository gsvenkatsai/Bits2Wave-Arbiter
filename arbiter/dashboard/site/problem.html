THE 2 AM COLLISION.

In autonomous cellular networks, nocturnal low-traffic periods trigger concurrent conflicting actions across decoupled control loops: Energy Saving (ES) attempts to switch lightly loaded cells into deep sleep, Load Balancing (LB) shifts user traffic to neighbors to relieve local congestion, and Coverage & Capacity Optimization (CC) boosts antenna power to eliminate fringe blind spots. Without mutual situational awareness, these independent controllers issue simultaneous conflicting commands at 2 AM—causing uncoordinated offloads into sleeping cells, immediate call drops, and severe RF coverage holes.

Conflict Classification

Project ARBITER models multi-controller interactions using the formal taxonomy established by the O-RAN Alliance.

Category 01

Direct Conflicts

Two or more controllers concurrently issue commands that modify the exact same configuration parameter on the same cell or network element within an overlapping execution window.

Example: ES requests Cell B administrative state = SHUTDOWN while CC concurrently requests Tx Power = +3 dB on Cell B.
Category 02

Indirect Conflicts

Controllers modify different parameters across adjacent cells that are physically or mathematically coupled through RF propagation, inter-cell interference, or user handover mobility.

Example: Cell A offloads 40% of its user pool to Cell B at the exact moment Cell B enters low-power standby mode.
Category 03

Implicit & Ping-Pong Conflicts

Multi-tick temporal coupling where Controller 1's action alters network state such that it immediately triggers Controller 2's threshold in the subsequent cycle, causing oscillatory ping-pong loops.

Example: LB offload pushes Cell B load above threshold, prompting immediate reverse handover or premature sleep reactivation.
Standardization Reference: Conflict modalities are modeled following the formal taxonomy defined in O-RAN Working Group 3 (WG3) Non-RT and Near-RT RIC Architecture and Multi-Controller Conflict Mitigation Framework.

Related Work & What ARBITER Adds

How ARBITER relates to existing conflict-mitigation research and the concrete capabilities implemented in this project.

O-RAN Conflict Mitigation Framework (CMF)

O-RAN WG3 defines architectural interfaces and coordination policies for Non-RT and Near-RT RICs. Standard CMF approaches typically rely on static priority matrices, parameter locking, or exclusion lists that serialize controller actions without joint optimization.

Digital-Twin Pre-Checks (COMIX & Related Works)

Academic proposals like COMIX evaluate proposed actions inside a simulation environment prior to deployment. These frameworks demonstrate the utility of pre-testing but typically treat simulation output as point estimates without formal uncertainty quantification.

Vendor SMO Conflict Management

Commercial Service Management and Orchestration platforms incorporate proprietary SON coordination rules. These systems excel at operational scheduling but frequently lack distribution-free mathematical safety guarantees or reproducible counterfactual audit trails.
